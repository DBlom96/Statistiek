\enquestion{8.29}{
    A postmix beverage machine is adjusted to release a certain amount of syrup into a chamber where it is mixed with carbonated water.
    A random sample of $25$ beverages was found to have a mean syrup content of $\bar{x} = 30\text{ mL}$ and a standard deviation of $s = 0.5\text{ mL}$.
    Find a \SI{95}{\percent} CI on the mean volume of syrup dispensed.
}
\enanswer{
    Whenever we want a two-sided $100 \cdot (1 - \alpha)$ \% confidence interval for the mean $\mu$ (in case $\sigma$ is unknown), we use the formula
    \[
        [\overline{x} - t_{\alpha/2; n-1} \cdot \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}, \overline{x} + t_{\alpha/2; n-1} \cdot \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}]
    \]
    Notice that we also do not have access to the value of $\sigma$, so we also have to estimate $\sigma$ with the sample standard deviation $s$.
    Here, if we want a \SI{95}{\percent} confidence interval, that means $100 \cdot (1 - \alpha) = 95$, or $\alpha = 0.05$.
    The corresponding value of $t_{\alpha/2; n-1}$ can be found by taking the $1-\alpha/2$-percentile of the $t$-distribution with
    $\df=n-1=24$ degrees of freedom:
    \[
        t_{\alpha/2; n-1} = t_{0.025; 24} = \invt(\opp=1-\alpha/2 = 0.975, \df=24) \approx 2.0639.
    \]
    Filling in $\overline{x} = 30$, $s = 0.5$, $n = 25$ and $\alpha = 0.05$ gives $[29.7936, 30.2064]$.
}

\enquestion{8.37}{
    The compressive strength of concrete is being tested by a civil engineer who tests $10$ specimens and obtains the following data:
    
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}{*{4}{C{0.225\textwidth}}}
            $2216$ & $2237$ & $2249$ & $2204$ \\
            $2225$ & $2301$ & $2281$ & $2263$ \\
            $2318$ & $2255$ & &
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item Check the assumption that compressive strength is normally distributed.
        Include a graphical display in your answer.
        \item Construct a \SI{95}{\percent} two-sided confidence interval on the mean strength.
        \item Construct a \SI{95}{\percent} lower confidence bound on the mean strength.
        Compare this bound with the lower bound of the two-sided confidence interval and discuss why they are different.
    \end{enumerate}
}
\enanswer{
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item Normal probability plot:
        \begin{center}
            \includegraphics[scale=0.7]{../../figures/opg8.37a.png}
        \end{center}
        The points lie reasonably close to a straight line, indicating that the compressive strength data can be assumed to be normally distributed.
        \item First, we need to compute the sample mean $\overline{x}$ and the sample standard deviation $s$:
        \begin{align*}
            \overline{x} = \frac{x_1+x_2+\ldots+x_n}{n} = \frac{ 2216 + 2237 + \ldots + 2255 }{ 10 } \approx 2254.9000.
        \end{align*}
    

        To compute the sample standard deviation $s$, we first compute the sample variance $s^2$:
        \begin{align*}
            s^2 &= \frac{ (x_1 - \overline{x})^2 + (x_2 - \overline{x})^2 + \ldots + (x_n - \overline{x})^2 }{ n - 1 } \\
                &= \frac{ (2216 - 2254.9000)^2 + (2237 - 2254.9000)^2 + \ldots + (2255 - 2254.9000)^2 }{ 10 - 1 } \\
                &\approx 1356.3222.
        \end{align*}
    

        The sample standard deviation $s$ is obtained by taking the square root of the sample variance $s^2$:
        \begin{align*}
            s = \sqrt{ s^2 } = \sqrt{ 1356.3222 } \approx 36.8283.
        \end{align*}
        Filling in $\overline{x} = 2254.9$, $s = 36.8283$, $n = 10$ and $\alpha = 0.05$ gives $[2228.5546, 2281.2454]$.
        \item For a lower confidence bound on the mean strength, we can compute a different type of interval.
        This time it is not symmetric around the sample mean $\overline{x}$, but using the following formula:
        \[
            [\overline{x} - t_{\alpha; n-1} \cdot \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}, \infty)
        \]
        Notice the difference in the subscript for the $t$-value: we now use the $\alpha$-th percentile instead of the $\alpha/2$-th percentile.
        Filling in $\overline{x} = 2254.9$, $s = 36.8283$, $n = 10$ and $\alpha = 0.05$ gives $[2233.5513,\infty)$.
    \end{enumerate}
}

\enquestion{8.41}{
    An article in \textit{Nuclear Engineering International} (February 1988, p.~33) describes several characteristics of fuel rods used in a reactor owned by an electric utility in Norway.
    Measurements on the percentage of enrichment of $12$ rods were reported as follows:
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}{*{6}{C{0.14\textwidth}}}
            $2.94$ & $3.00$ & $2.90$ & $2.75$ & $3.00$ & $2.95$ \\
            $2.90$ & $2.75$ & $2.95$ & $2.82$ & $2.81$ & $3.05$
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item Use a normal probability plot to check the normality assumption.
        \item Find a \SI{99}{\percent} two-sided confidence interval on the mean percentage of enrichment.
        Are you comfortable with the statement that the mean percentage of enrichment is \SI{2.95}{\percent}?
        Why?
    \end{enumerate}
}
\enanswer{
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item The data appear to be normally distributed.
        \item First, we need to compute the sample mean $\overline{x}$ and the sample standard deviation $s$:
        \begin{align*}
            \overline{x} = \frac{x_1+x_2+\ldots+x_n}{n} = \frac{ 2.94 + 3.0 + \ldots + 3.05 }{ 12 } \approx 2.9017.
        \end{align*}
    

        To compute the sample standard deviation $s$, we first compute the sample variance $s^2$:
        \begin{align*}
            s^2 &= \frac{ (x_1 - \overline{x})^2 + (x_2 - \overline{x})^2 + \ldots + (x_n - \overline{x})^2 }{ n - 1 } \\
                &= \frac{ (2.94 - 2.9017)^2 + (3.0 - 2.9017)^2 + \ldots + (3.05 - 2.9017)^2 }{ 12 - 1 } \\
                &\approx 0.0099.
        \end{align*}
    

        The sample standard deviation $s$ is obtained by taking the square root of the sample variance $s^2$:
        \begin{align*}
            s = \sqrt{ s^2 } = \sqrt{ 0.0099 } \approx 0.0993.
        \end{align*}
        Filling in $\overline{x} = 2.9017$, $s = 0.0993$, $n = 12$ and $\alpha = 0.01$ gives $[2.8126, 2.9907]$.
        
        We are comfortable with the statement that the mean percentage of enrichment is \SI{2.95}{\percent},
        as this value lies inside the confidence interval.
    \end{enumerate}
}
